# Post prompt template

Rules for generating 6AMKICK posts. The live prompt is in the n8n workflow, so copy any
change made here into that workflow too; this file is the reviewed reference copy.

## News posts (real athletes, coaches, races, results, records)

These are hard rules. A post that breaks one should not be published.

1. **Use only what the source says.** Never invent scenes, decisions, thoughts,
   feelings, settings, weather, splits, times, dates, quotes or other numbers that
   are not in the source article. Phrases like "faced a critical decision",
   "one spring evening" or "as his rivals began to tire" are invented unless the
   source says so.
2. **Quotes must be verbatim and attributed.** Only quote words that appear in the
   source, exactly as written, naming who said them and where. If the source has no
   quote, the post has no quote.
3. **Name the right people and events.** Use full names as the source gives them,
   and the correct race, meeting, date and result. Don't mix facts from different
   races (for example, a marathon course record in a half marathon story).
4. **Say exactly what kind of record it is.** Indoor, road or track; world, American
   or national; record, world best or course record.
5. **Lead the title with the athlete(s) and the event or result.** For example
   "Josh Hoey Sets 800m World Indoor Record: 1:42.50". The title must be 50
   characters or fewer, because " | 6AMKICK" is added to make the page title
   (60 characters maximum).
6. **Make the meta description factual.** Say who did what, where and when, plus the
   training angle, in 155 characters or fewer.
7. **Always link the source.** End the post with a "Source:" line linking to the
   original report (and to the official results page when one is used).
8. **Keep facts and advice separate.** The training takeaway for readers is advice,
   not a claim about the athlete. Don't attribute advice, motives or methods to an
   athlete unless the source does.
9. **If the source is too thin to support a post, don't write one.** A short,
   accurate post is better than a padded one.
